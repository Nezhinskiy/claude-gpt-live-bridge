# Review of PR #35: a reopened line the voice does not speak on closes after 5 s

- Pull request: [getLiveBridge/LiveBridge#35](https://github.com/getLiveBridge/LiveBridge/pull/35),
  `fix/silent-reopened-line-closes-early` → `dev`
- Head reviewed: `c43616c` (one commit), merge base `12f142e`. It merges cleanly onto `origin/dev`
  at `5a8fd93`, which holds PR #34's evidence that the docs cite.
- Skills: `superpowers:requesting-code-review`, `simplify` (report only, nothing applied),
  `security-review`
- Verification:
  - `scripts/check.sh` exits 0 on `c43616c`, and `swift test` passes 316 tests in 32 suites.
  - The findings below marked "probe" come from throwaway tests and mutations that were run and
    then removed; none of them is committed.
  - Not run against the real voice; `scripts/smoke.sh` was not run, as instructed.

## Verdict

**Merge after fixes.** It has no Critical findings and 2 Important ones: the owner's speech during
the connect is not counted (R1), and the evidence is overstated (R2). The other 6 are Minor. The
security pass found no vulnerabilities.

**Is it elegant?** Mostly yes. It is one branch in `(.connecting, .connected)`, and it reuses the
existing silence timer and `armSilenceTimer(seconds:)` effect. It adds no new timer, event or
Bridge code. The 5 s is measured against the same signal the probes logged as "the voice
speaking": the first assistant `transcript/delta`, which `RealtimeSession.swift:313` turns into
`.assistantSpeaking`. It is not measured against the lagging `transcript/done`. Two things keep it
from being fully clean:

- the duration is chosen outside `armSilence()`, so "which silence applies" now has two homes (S1);
- its guard, "nobody spoke", cannot see speech that starts while the line connects (R1).

**Is anything redundant?** No code is redundant. The `!isSoundOn` guard looks redundant, but it
is not: it can be false, though only through stale state today, and it becomes the real guard once
R1 is fixed. The "starts within about 2 s" fact is stated in four places, one more than the
documentation policy allows (S3).

## The checks asked for

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is 5 s safe against the measured start times? | Starts were measured in 10 runs, 0.7 to 2.0 s after `.connected`. Only 3 of those had no cue, the production condition (1.4, 0.8, 0.7 s). 5 s is 2.5× the slowest start and 3.5× the slowest start without a cue. It is defensible because a late start loses nothing (row 4), but the docs say it more firmly than n=3 allows (R2). |
| A slow connect | Safe. The timer is armed at `.connected`, after the connect, and `Bridge.run` arms it only after `flushPreroll` and every `addContext` have been awaited (`Bridge.swift:259`, `:277`). A slow connect does not eat into the 5 s, and the window even starts a little after `.connected`. |
| What counts as "the voice started"? Can it miss a start? | Any assistant `transcript/delta` sends `.assistantSpeaking`, which re-arms 20 s (`CallMachine.swift:194-197`, test `theVoiceSpeakingOnAnOpenedLineGetsTheUsualSilence`). The probes used this same signal, so the measurement and the rule agree. A delta that comes after the 5 s close lands in `.listening` and is ignored: the voice is cut off and the answer chimes. Audio that plays ahead of its first delta was not measured. If it ever does, the owner hears a word or two before the close. |
| The owner speaks | It is right on a live line: `speechStarted` arms 90 s, and `speechEnded` and `userTranscribed` arm 20 s. It is **not** right while the line connects: that `speechStarted` is dropped, so the 5 s can close the line on the owner mid-sentence (R1). |
| A second answer arrives | While the line connects, it is queued and becomes context at the connect. Once the line is live, an answer for a session nobody awaits re-arms 20 s (`CallMachine.swift:182-186`), so a silent line can stay open up to 20 s again (R5). |
| A target change, Stop, a remote close | Each one ends the wait: `conversationChanged` gives `closeRealtime` and `hangUp()`, Stop resets the machine, and `remoteClosed` gives `hangUp()`. A leftover timer is ignored outside `.live` and is replaced by the next connect. |
| Is a chimed answer kept unheard and said later? | Yes. `hangUp()` keeps `unheard` and chimes (`CallMachine.swift:260-272`, test `anOpenedLineLeftSilentChimes`). The next line gets it as context at its connect (`:178`). The reviewer probed the close, a late start, a target change and a remote close during the wait, and each one kept the answer pending. |
| Billing | The arithmetic holds. A silent reopening bills about 15 s less, about $0.0125 at $0.05 a minute. A spoken line is unchanged. A wrong early close costs one later reopening when the owner speaks, and that line would have been billed anyway. |
| A parallel timer where the silence timer could take a parameter? | No parallel timer. The duration is built inline next to `armSilence()` rather than by it (S1). |

## superpowers:requesting-code-review

### Important

**R1. The owner speaking while a reopened line connects is cut off after 5 s.**

- Where: `app/Sources/BridgeCore/Call/CallMachine.swift:156-159`, `:171-176`.
- The problem:
  - `(.connecting, .speechStarted)` has no case and falls to `default`, so `isSoundOn` stays
    false.
  - At `.connected`, `isOpeningForResult && !isSoundOn` holds, so the line arms 5 s.
  - Local voice detection sends no second `speechStarted` while the sound goes on, and
    `userTranscribed` comes only once an utterance is done.
  - So an owner who starts speaking during the 1.3 to 3.1 s connect, and is still speaking 5 s
    after it, gets `.silenceElapsed`. With `awaited` empty, that means `closeRealtime` and a chime
    over their words.
- Probe: given `[.start, opening(result), .speechStarted]`, `.connected` gives
  `armSilenceTimer(seconds: 5)`, and a following `.silenceElapsed` gives
  `[.closeRealtime, .chime]`, ending in `.listening`.
- Why this PR: the gap is older than the PR, but the 20 s rule hid it. A reopening is exactly when
  the owner is likely to start talking ("so, what happened?"). The comment at `:173` ("a line
  nobody spoke on") states the intent, but the code does not meet it.
- Suggested fix:
  - Write the failing row first, for example `theOwnerSpeakingWhileAnOpenedLineConnectsGetsTheNoiseCap`:
    given `[.start, opening(result), .speechStarted]`, the event `.connected` should give
    `[.flushPreroll, .addContext(result), cap, .problem(nil)]`.
  - Then add `case (.connecting, .speechStarted): isSoundOn = true; return []`.
  - The existing guard then picks `armSilence()`, which gives 90 s while the sound is on, as on a
    line speech opened, and 20 s after `speechEnded`.
  - This also gives the `!isSoundOn` guard the test it lacks (R3).

**R2. The PR body overstates the evidence, and two docs repeat the claim as a guarantee.**

- Where:
  - the PR body ("13 runs that spoke, cue or not");
  - `CHANGELOG.md:11-15` ("when the voice speaks on such a line at all, it starts within about 2
    seconds");
  - `docs/architecture/overview.md:220-223` ("A voice that has not started after
    `CallMachine.firstWordSeconds` (5 s) will not").
- The problem: in item (n) on `origin/dev`, only the two cue tables measured the *start* of
  speech, which makes 10 runs that spoke.
  - **Without a cue:** Control 2, 3 and 5, at 1.4, 0.8 and 0.7 s. Production sends no cue.
  - **With a cue:** the other 7, at 0.8 to 2.0 s.
  - **The "13":** it seems to add runs 7, 10 and 11, which measured the end of the first segment
    and all had the "Any news?" nudge.
  - **The other runs without a cue:** they never measured a start, only first segments ending at
    5.4 to 10.5 s.
  - **Pooling the two groups:** the ADR's case for it is plausible but inferred: the voice often
    started before the cue's transcript arrived.
  - So the slowest start measured in production conditions is 1.4 s, out of 3 runs.
  - "Will not" and "starts within about 2 seconds" read as facts about the voice. Those 3 runs
    support "in the runs measured". AGENTS.md says docs never claim more than the code enforces,
    and the same holds for the evidence.
- Suggested fix:
  - Correct the PR body: 10 timed runs, 3 of them without a cue.
  - Phrase the CHANGELOG and the overview as an observation, with the safety net beside it. For
    example: "in the runs measured, the voice started within about 2 s or not at all; a voice that
    starts later is cut off, and the answer chimes and waits".
  - Give the same count in the ADR amendment (`docs/adr/0001-platform-findings.md:431-434`).

### Minor

**R3. The new test rows do not pin the behaviour.**

- Where: `app/Tests/BridgeCoreTests/CallMachineTests.swift:24`, `:91-93`.
- The problem:
  - `firstWord` is built from `CallMachine.firstWordSeconds`, while its siblings `arm` (20) and
    `cap` (90) hard-code their values. The mutation `firstWordSeconds = 20` passes the whole suite.
  - Probe: deleting `&& !isSoundOn` also passes all 77 transition rows. The guard is reachable,
    because a hang-up with the sound on leaves `isSoundOn` true into `.listening`, but no row
    reaches it.
- Suggested fix:
  - `static let firstWord = CallEffect.armSilenceTimer(seconds: 5)`, like its siblings.
  - The R1 row covers the guard.

**R4. The PR body calls both new rows failing-first. One of them passes on the base.**

- Where: `app/Tests/BridgeCoreTests/CallMachineTests.swift:94-97`
  (`theVoiceSpeakingOnAnOpenedLineGetsTheUsualSilence`).
- The problem: on `12f142e`, `(.live, .assistantSpeaking)` already returned `[armSilence()]` (20 s).
  The row is a useful guard, but it is not a failing test before the change.
- Suggested fix: keep the row, and correct the PR body.

**R5. A second answer during the 5 s wait brings back the 20 s.**

- Where: `app/Sources/BridgeCore/Call/CallMachine.swift:182-186`.
- The problem:
  - Probe: on a reopened line after `.connected`, a delivery for a session nobody awaits gives
    `[.addContext(b), armSilenceTimer(seconds: 20)]`.
  - A line that is still silent then stays open until 20 s after that answer, while the README
    says it "closes after 5 seconds" (`README.md:377`). The efficiency angle of simplify found the
    same billing cost.
  - It is defensible: nobody has measured how fast the voice starts on context added to a live
    line, so 20 s is the cautious choice.
- Suggested fix, either of:
  - say it in the overview's closed-line section;
  - or make "awaiting the first word" state that `armSilence()` consults (S1, second form).
  - The first is enough unless it shows up in use.

### Minor

**S1. The duration is chosen next to `armSilence()`, not by it.** (Reuse, simplification and
altitude all found this.)

- Where: `app/Sources/BridgeCore/Call/CallMachine.swift:131-134`, `:173-176`.
- The problem:
  - Every other silence timer goes through `armSilence()`, which also clears `isSilenceDue`. This
    one builds `CallEffect.armSilenceTimer` inline and skips the reset.
  - That is harmless today, since `hangUp()` and `end()` leave `isSilenceDue` false before any
    connect. But a later change to `armSilence()` would miss this path.
  - The `isSoundOn` choice is also split across two places: the ternary and the helper.
- Suggested fix, smallest form: give the helper the quiet duration as a parameter.

  ```swift
  private mutating func armSilence(quiet: Int = Self.silenceSeconds) -> CallEffect {
    isSilenceDue = false
    return .armSilenceTimer(seconds: isSoundOn ? Self.noiseCapSeconds : quiet)
  }
  ```

  The connect then reads `armSilence(quiet: isOpeningForResult ? Self.firstWordSeconds :
  Self.silenceSeconds)`. With R1 fixed, `isSoundOn` still wins, so `!isSoundOn` leaves the call
  site, and so do the local `silence` binding and the comment at `:173`.
- Second form (altitude): a flag, `isAwaitingFirstWord`, that `armSilence()` consults.
  - Set it at the connect.
  - Clear it on `.assistantSpeaking`, `.speechStarted`, `.userTranscribed` and in `hangUp()`.
  - This also settles R5. It is about the same size, but it adds a state flag. Prefer it only if
    R5 is to be fixed in code.

**S2. The comment and the guard describe a case the code cannot detect.**

- Where: `app/Sources/BridgeCore/Call/CallMachine.swift:173-175`.
- The problem:
  - "a line nobody spoke on" with `!isSoundOn` reads as "the owner has not spoken". Today
    `isSoundOn` can be true here only when it is left over from a hang-up with the sound on. A
    reader cannot tell what the condition protects.
  - In that leftover case, the line gets the 90 s noise cap although nobody may be speaking. That
    is older than the PR, since `hangUp()` does not reset `isSoundOn`.
- Suggested fix: the R1 fix makes the comment true. Leaving `isSoundOn` alone in `hangUp()` is
  right, because local voice detection still owes its `speechEnded`.

**S3. "Starts within about 2 s" is stated in four places.**

- Where:
  - `CallMachine.swift:117-119`;
  - `docs/architecture/overview.md:220`;
  - `docs/adr/0001-platform-findings.md:432-433`;
  - `CHANGELOG.md:13`.
- The problem:
  - The measurement has its home in ADR 0001 (n). The amendment is the decision that cites it, and
    the CHANGELOG is history.
  - The doc comment and the overview copy the number. "Once the voice speaks, the 20 s rule
    applies" is also restated in the doc comment's neighbourhood, the overview and the ADR.
  - The PR itself had to replace the overview's old "about 8 s", so the next measurement means
    editing four files.
  - This breaks [one fact, one home](../architecture/documentation.md).
- Suggested fix:
  - Doc comment: "How long a line a result opened waits for the voice's first word (ADR 0001 (n))."
  - Overview: name `CallMachine.firstWordSeconds`, link item (n) for the number, and keep the
    behaviour.
  - Leave the CHANGELOG as it is, apart from R2's rewording.

### Considered and not raised

- Efficiency: every assistant delta re-arms the timer, which means a cancelled `Task` and a
  `changed()` call. That is older than the PR, it does not affect billing, and skipping the re-arm
  would close long replies early.
- The reuse angle found nothing else to flag: the constant sits with its siblings, and the rows use
  the `row(...)` helper.

## security-review

No findings.

- The change only shortens how long the microphone and the realtime line stay open.
  `firstWordSeconds` is a compile-time constant that no input reaches, and no path leaves a line
  open longer than before.
- It adds no handoff, permission or wire effect. LiveBridge still never answers a Claude Code
  permission prompt ([SECURITY.md](../../SECURITY.md)).
- `unheard`, `awaited` and `rename` are untouched, and the early close goes through `hangUp()`. No
  session's data reaches another session.
- No logging, secrets or new socket surface.

## Considered and declined

- **A cue instead of a timeout:** the owner decided this on 2026-11-05, and the ADR records it.
- **Whether 3 timed starts without a cue are enough:** this is a product call, and only the
  wording of the claims is raised (R2).
- **Actor re-entrancy at the connect:** the 5 s arm runs after the `addContext` awaits, so an
  `.assistantSpeaking` that interleaves there is overridden by the later arm.
  - The next delta re-arms 20 s, so the voice is not cut off while it speaks.
  - Narrow case: if a short reply's deltas and its `transcript/done` all land inside those awaits,
    the owner gets 5 s, not 20 s, to answer. That needs two or more pending lines.
  - The ordering is older than the PR, and the PR only changes the stakes.
- **`withdraw` closing a connecting result line while the owner speaks**
  (`CallMachine.swift:240-244`): the same blind spot as R1, but older than the PR and outside it.
  The R1 fix makes a later guard there possible.
- **The six translations:** each was checked only for changing 20 to 5, not for wording.
